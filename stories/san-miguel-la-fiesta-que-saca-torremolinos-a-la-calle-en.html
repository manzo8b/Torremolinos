San Miguel: the festival that brings Torremolinos into the streets

Pilgrimage, fair, procession and collective memory: a September story that belongs to the whole town

TORREMOLINOS editorial team2 November 20267 min readTranslated from Spanish
EspañolEnglishItalianoDeutschFrançaisРусский

Some celebrations are remembered for their music, others for the journey people make, and others because they transform the familiar townscape for a few days. San Miguel brings those experiences together, but each has its own time and meaning.

A town that recognises itself in its streets

Every September, Torremolinos changes rhythm. The town so often presented through its beaches and nightlife reveals another dimension: popular festivals, neighbourhood gatherings and traditions that still occupy public space.

The San Miguel Romería and Feria are not a single activity. The municipal tourism portal distinguishes the pilgrimage, the daytime fair in the centre and the evening programme at the fairground. They form part of the same festive cycle, but do not represent the same day, participants or meaning. [1]

Understanding this difference allows visitors to enjoy the celebration without turning it into a generic postcard. The festivities speak of the town, its generations and the relationship between residents and their streets.

The Romería: journey, music and encounters

The San Miguel Romería has its own identity: on the Sunday before the fair, numerous participants travel from the centre to the San Miguel chapel in Los Pinares. According to municipal information, they gather on foot and in carts, with music, singing and culinary traditions. [1][3]

The route is a collective celebration that takes place both along the journey and at the gathering. It is important to distinguish it from the religious procession associated with the patron saint's day: although they share a devotional reference, they are different events.

The town council describes it as one of the most important urban pilgrimages in Andalusia. That description belongs to the institutional source; it is not an independent ranking by this editorial team. [1]

The Feria: the centre by day, the fairground by night

During the Feria, the streets of the centre become spaces for music, food and social gatherings. The evening programme moves to the fairground, with its own distinctive character. [1]

A first-time visitor to Torremolinos may encounter very different atmospheres depending on the time and place. The pedestrian centre can be a daytime meeting point; later, the fairground hosts some of the shows and attractions.

There is no single way to take part: some people seek the music and atmosphere, others come with children or reconnect with a tradition they have known for years. Logistics also deserve attention: mobility, accessibility and a safe journey home, especially in crowds. Details change with each edition and require specific checks.

A festival does not turn the street into a stage by accident: the town prepares it, shares it and remembers it year after year.

29 September: Saint Michael the Archangel

Saint Michael the Archangel is the patron saint of Torremolinos. The procession on 29 September belongs to the town's festive and religious calendar. The official 2024 programme describes a departure from San Miguel church and a route through several central streets. [2]

This illustrates why memory must be documented precisely: the 2024 route does not establish that times or streets were identical in 2025 or 2026, or that they will be in 2027.

Photographs of parades and processions supplied to the TORREMOLINOS archive are valuable visual records. For now they remain off this page: before publishing them we must confirm the edition to which they belong and assess the image rights of recognisable people.

Dates: what happened in 2026 and what remains unconfirmed

The official tourism calendar published for 2026 the Romería on Sunday 20 September and the Feria from 25 to 29 September. These are past dates, not a future programme. [1]

This publication does not announce 2027 dates or live schedules. When the next programme is released, TORREMOLINOS can link to the institutional calendar and prepare a practical guide to routes, accessibility, public transport and updated recommendations.

For current events, readers will find links in the guide to the official tourism calendar and the Culture Department's programme. A responsible page distinguishes history, which remains, from schedules, prices and activities that can change. [4]

A festival that speaks of the town

The San Miguel Feria encapsulates an important part of Torremolinos: its religious traditions, music scene, cuisine and the ways residents and visitors share everyday places.

This is a story about the context of the celebration, not a commercial calendar or a reconstruction of other people's testimony. We do not attribute statements to participants or turn photographs without confirmed dates into evidence of a particular edition.

Torremolinos is sea, nightlife and diversity. It is also the town that takes to the streets to recognise itself in a shared celebration.
