La Nogalera, where the night has a history

A meeting place in the heart of Torremolinos, where everyday life, LGBTQ+ culture and a memory that should not be erased come together

A meeting place in the heart of Torremolinos, where everyday life, LGBTQ+ culture and a memory that should not be erased come together.

A square that stays alive after the party ends

In the heart of Torremolinos, La Nogalera is one of those names that comes up again and again in conversations about the town. The official tourism portal describes it as the epicentre of nightlife, a pedestrian square connected to streets, shops, bars and restaurants. The suburban railway station nearby provides connections to Málaga, the airport and other coastal towns. [1]

The area is known for its cosmopolitan character, particularly its importance to the LGBTQ+ community. But describing it exclusively as a place to party would be inadequate: its life also includes daytime strolls, encounters, everyday work, terraces and shops. These overlapping rhythms explain much of its appeal.

Diversity has a memory

In the 1960s, Torremolinos brought together social spaces that were unusual in Francoist Spain. The municipal tourism blog documents the opening of Toni's Bar in Pasaje Begoña in 1962, describing it as one of the country's earliest gay venues. It also recalls Pourquoi pas?, which opened in La Nogalera in 1968 and is regarded as a pioneer of lesbian nightlife. [2]

This history does not mean that freedom was guaranteed. On 24 June 1971, a major police raid in Pasaje Begoña and nearby areas had a profound impact on people and businesses. Understanding that past prevents us from mistaking today's tourist visibility for a history that was always simple or linear. [3]

La Nogalera is not defined by a single night: it is also shaped by the people who live there every day.

A broad scene, without a single label

La Nogalera brings together diverse traditions and audiences. It is important to avoid simplistic descriptions of the age, identity or orientation of people visiting its venues. It would also be unreliable to claim that a venue is open, has a particular atmosphere or costs a specific amount without recent verification.

How to discover it respectfully

A good way to approach the neighbourhood is to stroll through the centre and adjacent streets at an unhurried pace, observing how the atmosphere changes between afternoon and night. Always consult each venue's official channels before planning a night out: opening hours, programmes and entry conditions can change.

The festive atmosphere does not remove basic rules of coexistence: respect neighbours' rest, ask permission before photographing recognisable people and use suitable transport for the return journey. Celebrating diversity also means caring for shared spaces.

What to visit nearby

Pasaje Begoña: a place of historical memory essential to understanding the relationship between the town and LGBTQ+ rights. [3]

Calle San Miguel: the central pedestrian shopping street, connecting several squares and lively streets. [4]

Pueblo Blanco: a nearby complex that the municipal portal associates with dining and terraces in the central area. [3]
