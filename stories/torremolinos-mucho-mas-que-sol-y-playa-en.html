Torremolinos, much more than sun and beach

A town with an old mill in its name, the Mediterranean in its everyday life and a history of freedom worth discovering

A town with an old mill in its name, the Mediterranean in its everyday life and a history of freedom worth discovering.

A town that had a name long before tourism

Torremolinos did not begin when the first tourists arrived. Its name is linked to the old Torre de los Molinos, now called Torre Pimentel, built in the Nasrid period around 1300. The tower watched over the coast from an elevated position, alongside the mills that formed part of the local economy. It still stands near Calle San Miguel and Cuesta del Tajo, in the town centre. [1]

The growth of tourism in the twentieth century radically transformed the municipality, but did not entirely erase that geography. Walking from the streets of the centre towards El Bajondillo reveals something essential: the town and the beach occupy different levels. The terrain affects routes, walking times and accessibility.

It is worth starting by recognising these differences in elevation. A genuinely useful guide should not promise an easy walk for everyone without explaining the slopes, or recommend lifts without checking that they work.

La Carihuela, the memory of a fishing town

La Carihuela preserves a particularly expressive part of the local identity. The municipal tourism portal recalls that its residents traditionally depended on fish and seafood, before tourism growth from the 1950s encouraged restaurants, accommodation and beach bars. [2]

That transformation can be seen in the promenade, the narrow streets and the historic establishments. Fish dishes, espetos and pescaíto frito are part of a culinary offering that coexists with an increasingly international town. Yet reducing the neighbourhood to a list of places to eat would be a mistake: its humble origins, ways of living on the coast and the continuity of its memories also matter.

You do not have to discover everything in one day. Torremolinos is better understood when you stop looking only towards the sea.

The other story: an open and also contradictory town

Torremolinos has been associated with freedom and diversity since its tourist expansion. Pasaje Begoña and La Nogalera are central to that memory. The former was the scene of the so-called great police raid on 24 June 1971, when Francoist repression struck people and establishments associated with nightlife and sexual diversity. [3]

LGBTQ+ history cannot be relegated to a footnote in the guide. Nor should it become decoration or a slogan: it deserves verifiable dates, authentic testimony, identifiable sources and respect for those who lived through those years. The present vitality of La Nogalera is better understood in light of that historical dimension.

A town to stay in, not just visit

Residents need different information from someone arriving on a Friday: train connections, services, accessible routes, facilities and neighbourhood news. First-time visitors may be looking for a good beach, authentic food or somewhere to go out at night. Both perspectives have a place in TORREMOLINOS.

That is why this publication aims to combine lasting stories with practical information that can be reviewed. Neither an accommodation catalogue disguised as a magazine nor an endless list of undated recommendations. The ambition is to tell the town's story with curiosity, precision and a sense of community.

Three ways to begin discovering the town

Through history: start at Torre Pimentel, explore the centre and Cuesta del Tajo, checking beach access beforehand.

Through the sea: walk through La Carihuela, observe its urban layout and continue towards Punta or Morro de Torremolinos, one of the natural landmarks that shape the coastline. [2]

Through memory: discover the history of Pasaje Begoña before exploring La Nogalera. This is not a shopping itinerary; it is an invitation to look at the town more deeply.
